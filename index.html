ENERGY IN,ENERGY OUT

Drop something and watch potential energy turn into kinetic energy and back. With bounciness at 1 and no air drag the total stays flat. Anything less and every bounce leaks energy as heat.

In zero gravity, momentum is conserved in every collision, whatever the bounciness. Try two balls head-on.

A PHYSICS ENGINE,FROM SCRATCH

Everything here runs on a small rigid-body engine written for this page. No libraries. Each frame goes through the same four steps, several times over.

01Integrate

Gravity and drag change each velocity, v += g dt, then positions move, x += v dt. Each frame is split into at least 4 smaller steps.

02Detect collisions

Circles use distance. Polygons use the separating axis test, then clip the touching edges to find up to two contact points.

03Solve contacts

Sequential impulses push bodies apart along the contact normal, with bounce e and a friction limit μ N. Impulses are remembered between steps so stacks stay still.

04Solve joints

Hinges pin points together. Rods hold a distance. Ropes only pull. Springs are soft constraints with a frequency and a damping ratio.

Limits: 2D only, no sleeping, and no continuous collision detection, so very fast thin objects can tunnel through walls. Spring energy is not counted in the energy readouts. Not intended for engineering analysis.
